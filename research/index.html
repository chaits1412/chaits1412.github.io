M.Sc. Research Project Unsupervised learning 2024–25

Customer Segmentation using the RFM Model and K-Means

Dept. of Information Technology, St. Xavier's College, Mumbai (Autonomous) · with Sejal Gawade

Question

Can raw transaction records be reduced to a customer segmentation that is both statistically defensible and specific enough to act on?

Method, as run

Transaction log→Aggregate per customer→ RFM features→Log transform→ Min–Max scaling→K-means→Elbow selection

Transactions were aggregated per CustomerID into Recency (days since last purchase), Frequency (transaction count) and Monetary (total spend). The analysis was restricted to UK customers, who dominate the dataset — that reduces noise from thinly-represented regions, at the cost of generalisability. A log transform addressed the heavy right skew typical of transactional spend, and Min–Max scaling stopped Monetary dominating the distance metric purely through its units.

I ran two segmentations deliberately. First, a rule-based RFM quintile score — each customer scored 1–5 per dimension, concatenated into codes like 555 (Top Customers) or 111 (Lost). Second, K-means on the transformed features. One is interpretable by construction; the other is learned. Reading them against each other is what made the limitations visible.

Results

Three segments emerged and read without straining: at-risk (infrequent, low-spend, long since seen), potential loyalists (moderate on every dimension) and loyal (frequent, high-spend, recent). The rule-based RFM codes broadly agreed at the extremes — top and lost customers landed where you would expect — and disagreed in the middle, which is the part worth attention.

What this does not establish

  • The elbow is a heuristic, not a criterion. K = 3 is where the curve bends, not where a statistic says the partition is best. No silhouette or gap statistic was computed, so validity is argued visually rather than measured.
  • K-means assumes what the data does not supply. It seeks roughly spherical, similar-variance clusters under Euclidean distance. Log-RFM space is neither isotropic nor cleanly separable — the boundary is partly an artefact of forcing a hard partition onto a continuum.
  • Outliers pull centroids. A few very high-Monetary customers exert influence far out of proportion to their count, and K-means has no mechanism to set them aside.
  • Agreement between the two segmentations is not validation. Both are built from the same three features; they share their blind spots.

Where I would take it

Stop assuming a hard partition. Gaussian mixture models would give soft membership and let the middle of the distribution stay ambiguous rather than be adjudicated; DBSCAN would allow density-defined segments and, importantly, permit points to belong to nothing; hierarchical clustering would show whether the three-way split is stable or an accident of K. Beyond method, three aggregates discard the entire temporal structure of a purchase history — I suspect sequence matters more than the RFM framing allows.

M.Sc. Coursework Project Supervised learning 2024

Predicting Customer Churn: a comparison of five classifiers

M.Sc. Big Data Analytics · 10,000 bank customers, 14 raw attributes

Question

Which model family actually earns its complexity on tabular behavioural data — and what drives the prediction once you look inside?

Method, as run

Categorical attributes were one-hot encoded (Geography) or binarised (Gender), and identifier columns dropped. Because several numeric features were visibly skewed, I examined log, square-root, Box–Cox and Yeo–Johnson transforms before settling the feature set. Five classifiers were trained and evaluated on a held-out split — logistic regression as a baseline, then decision tree, gradient boosting, random forest and XGBoost — with the random forest tuned by randomised search over a 5-fold cross-validated grid.

Two things are worth drawing out. Age carries more signal than every financial attribute, which reframes churn here as a life-stage phenomenon rather than a purely economic one. And the tree ensembles separate the classes well — AUC above 0.93 — while logistic regression lands twelve points lower on accuracy, suggesting the decision boundary is genuinely non-linear rather than merely noisy.

What this does not establish

  • Tuning bought essentially nothing. Randomised search moved accuracy from 87.98% to 88.06% — eight hundredths of a percentage point, well inside run-to-run variance. Reported honestly, that is a null result, not an improvement.
  • The classes are imbalanced, so accuracy flatters every model. Churners are roughly a fifth of the sample; predicting "stays" for everyone would score near 80%. Recall and AUC carry the information here, and recall tops out at 0.81 — about one churner in five is still missed.
  • Impurity-based importance is biased toward continuous, high-cardinality features. Age is continuous and Gender is binary, so the gap between them is partly an artefact of the measure. Permutation importance or SHAP would be the honest test, and I did not run either.
  • No calibration was assessed. The scores rank well, which is what AUC measures, but nothing here shows the predicted probabilities mean what they say — and any cost-sensitive threshold depends on exactly that.

Where I would take it

Replace impurity importance with permutation and SHAP, assess probability calibration before trusting any threshold, and treat the imbalance directly — cost-sensitive learning or resampling — rather than reporting accuracy over it. The more interesting question underneath is whether the Age effect is a genuine life-stage signal or a proxy for something unmeasured, which needs data this dataset does not contain.

What I want to work on

Three threads, each traceable to a specific limitation I ran into — not a topic I found attractive from the outside.

Clustering under violated assumptions

Density-based and mixture models for behavioural data that is heavy-tailed, continuous at the boundaries, and full of legitimate outliers. When should a method be permitted to assign nothing at all?

From: K-means forcing a hard partition on log-RFM space

Interpretability that survives scrutiny

Feature attribution that holds up against the biases of the measure itself — permutation and SHAP over impurity — and calibration, so a predicted probability means what it claims.

From: Age dominating a ranking I could not fully trust
