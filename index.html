Data on Demand

A diagnostic analytics project on a simulated quick-commerce business, built to answer one question: where are the hidden operational gaps between what customers want and what the business actually delivers?

End-to-end analysis

Quick-Commerce Gap & Customer Experience Analysis

Real Zepto and Blinkit data isn't public, so I generated a realistic 150,000-search / 78,936-order dataset simulating the operational reality of a quick-commerce platform — customers, products, inventory, dark stores, deliveries, fees and support tickets — then investigated it as though it were live.

Python→ BigQuery SQL→ Root-cause analysis→ Power BI→ Recommendations
01

The product availability gap

54.28% of customer searches ended in Product Unavailable or Limited Options — only 44.32% found full availability. The single largest demand-supply gap in the dataset, concentrated in identifiable SKUs: Cola (78.4%), Sanitary Pads (75.9%) and Power Bank (71.3%).

02

Order accuracy by category

Wrong Item and Missing Item complaints clustered sharply. Women's Clothing drew the highest complaint count at 625 — nearly 50% more than the next category, Travel Essentials at 423.

03

Where support load actually sits

Tickets spread across six recurring issue types, with Refund Requests and Wrong Item complaints tied at 1,076 each. Cancellation rate was tested as a driver but showed too little variation to matter.

The finding I couldn't close

One dark store behaved unlike every other store in the network — and I could not explain why.

MetricDS009Network average
Avg. delivery time31.69 min22.12 min
Avg. delay12.03 min4.13 min
On-time rate6.13%~41–43%

I ran a full root-cause investigation across four candidate explanations — peak-hour load, distance to customer, order workload, and picking/packing time — as a chained sequence of SQL queries. All four were ruled out.

So I documented DS009 as an unresolved operational anomaly and flagged it for manual, on-the-ground investigation, rather than picking the most plausible-sounding cause and presenting it as an answer. A flagged unknown is more useful to a business than a confident wrong one.
